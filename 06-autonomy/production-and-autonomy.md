## Autonomy Dial by segment

The intended user segments are experienced PMs and PMs new to Cortex, keeping Cortex focused on PMs. Autonomy is a product decision per segment; it does not move the Module 1 agent line. External communications and commitments remain human-owned for both segments.

| Segment | Desired autonomy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Experienced PM | Supervised | I prefer supervised because communications, scope, and related decisions need HITL. After human approval of project context and tone, Cortex may retrieve evidence, draft, validate, and propose stories as a complete proposal. The PM reviews claims, scope, stories, and external communication before any external action; publication and commitments remain human-owned. |
| PM new to Cortex | Assisted | I chose assisted to help PMs new to Cortex understand the evidence and scope before drafting. The desired workflow adds human review of selected evidence and proposed scope before drafting, followed by review of both final outputs. Context/tone approval, claims checks, story approval, publication, and commitments retain their existing human boundaries. The additional intermediate review is a planned workflow, not an implemented checkpoint in the current runtime. |

## Trust Ladder

- **Current rung:** Supervised (local prototype).
  Cortex prepares and validates updates and story proposals within defined limits, while the PM reviews claims, scope, and both outputs before external action.
  Supervised fits because model and critic errors still require human judgment, and publication and commitments remain human-owned.
- **Eval gate to reach the next rung:** Move toward bounded-autonomous preparation only after a 6-week supervised evidence window with at least 30 supervised runs. Require at least 95% pass for each of M5 EV-1 (tool-call accuracy), EV-2 (path quality), EV-3 (recovery), and EV-4 (task completion), plus 100% pass on evaluated EV-5 safety cases and zero unauthorized actions or confidential disclosures. Exercise controlled recovery and jailbreak cases throughout the window, recording the numerator, denominator, and outcome for each eval; missing evidence means the gate is not met. I chose more time with the same thresholds so we are confident before moving to the next rung. This is a proposed gate, not achieved results; publication and commitments remain human-owned, and the additional assisted checkpoint for new PMs is still planned.
- **Incident record and clean-window policy:** Maintain a reviewed log of model/critic errors, near-misses, refusals, and bound stops, with date, run/source version, safe issue summary, classification, owner, corrective action, and closure evidence. Expected controlled-test refusals and bound stops are test outcomes rather than incidents; unexpected issues require review and closure before advancing. Any unauthorized action or confidential disclosure resets the clean 6-week window. I chose this policy to capture all types of possible issues. Evidence so far includes false critic rejections, unsupported wording, a caught injected metric, an initial jailbreak run that failed explicit refusal, the corrected runtime refusal, and demonstrated cost-threshold overshoot; no publication or tracker mutation occurred in those captured runs. A sustained 6-week clean record with at least 30 supervised runs has not been established. This logging/closure workflow is a governance plan, not an implemented production incident system.

## Deployment plan

- **Runtime:** Always-on service/container with scheduled and on-demand triggers. I chose this because I want Cortex to run on schedule as well as on demand. The service stays available, but each trigger starts a finite job using approved project context and tone, retaining the on-demand prototype's bounded loop: at most 5 drafts/4 revisions, 600 seconds per run, 60 seconds per model API request, $0.10 estimated per-run threshold, and 10 proposed stories. Prevent overlapping runs for the same project; retain HITL review and human-owned publication/commitments. This is a deployment plan, not a running service: scheduling, request handling, approved-context management, per-project concurrency control, and service infrastructure still require implementation. Per-run cost limits do not impose a service-wide daily spending cap.
- **Operator / on-call owner:** Primary operator: me, the learner and Cortex PM owner. I review outputs, triage escalations, maintain the incident record, and pause scheduled runs when needed. Technical backup: an engineering lead responsible for service failures, credentials, model/API outages, and rollback; the individual has not yet been named. Use a private review queue: scope/claims questions go to the PM owner, technical failures to the engineering backup, and suspected disclosures to the organization's security/privacy contact. The actual queue and security/privacy contact are not yet named or connected. This ownership split is agreed, but naming the backup/contact, selecting the destination, and implementing routing are production-readiness gaps. The current prototype holds outputs locally and sends no notifications.
- **Rollback:** Pause scheduled and on-demand triggers, stop active jobs, and restore a known-good approved version of code, prompts, and non-secret configuration. Reduce the autonomy dial when warranted, review held outputs separately, and run the relevant eval/replay checks before resuming. Keep a version record for each deployment and run. I chose this because it is a logical rollback process for Cortex. For the current CLI, the operator can stop the process and restore an approved Git version/configuration; service-wide trigger pause, active-job cancellation, and segment-specific dial controls still require implementation. Stopping local execution may not cancel an in-flight remote request. External publication/tracker writes are absent, so there are no Cortex-created external mutations to undo; saved artifacts must still be checked before reuse.
- **Monitoring:** Track EV-1–EV-5 pass rates with numerators/denominators and source/run versions; escalation reasons and rates; incident and near-miss counts with closure status; estimated run cost versus actual provider cost when available; duration, timeouts, and draft/revision counts; overlapping-run attempts. The PM owner reviews these signals during the 6-week supervised pilot and uses the agreed incident policy for investigation and closure. I chose the full set because it provides clear monitoring for Cortex. Current traces/results provide some local observations, but aggregate metrics, concurrency tracking, dashboards, production alerts, and provider-cost reconciliation still require implementation. Expected test refusals and bound stops must remain distinguishable from unexpected incidents.

## Widen-autonomy decision rule

Increase autonomy for an experienced-PM pilot only after 6 weeks with at least 30 supervised runs achieve at least 95% pass for each of EV-1–EV-4, 100% pass on evaluated EV-5 safety cases, zero unauthorized actions or confidential disclosures, review and closure of unexpected issues, and explicit PM-owner approval of the change. Missing evidence means no increase; a violation resets the clean window. Publication and commitments remain human-owned for everyone, and PMs new to Cortex remain assisted. This is the agreed future decision rule; the gate is not yet met and no segment-specific autonomy switch has been implemented.

## Governance & forward strategy

- **Compliance:** Use only minimum necessary, approved project data. Secrets, credentials, and unnecessary personal identifiers must not enter prompts or traces. Respect confidential/embargoed-project restrictions, scope access to approved projects and reviewers, redact sensitive trace content, and apply approved retention/deletion policies. Retain the M4 working/shared run scope, 60-day episodic lifetime, and 30-day semantic review as the agreed memory policies; saved artifacts require their own approved retention schedule. I chose this as the expected governance approach for Cortex. The build has scoped retrieval, some confidentiality checks, and safe static logging for explicit task overrides, but general PII/secret filtering, trace redaction, memory expiry, and artifact deletion are not fully implemented. Data classification, applicable organizational requirements, and the artifact retention schedule must be approved before production.
- **Safety:** Retain the M1/M5 human boundaries for every segment: approve context/tone, validate risks and choose escalation routing, spot-check claims, and approve both outputs and proposed stories. Publication, external commitments, ticket changes, and merges remain human-owned; Cortex has no publishing or tracker-write tools. A critic pass or queued proposal is not human approval. The operator can stop the local process, with iteration, timeout, and cost limits as automatic safeguards. The future service must support pausing all triggers and stopping active jobs; those service controls are not implemented yet, and a local stop may not cancel an in-flight remote request. The task preflight refuses known explicit permission/rule overrides without logging their raw text; it is not a universal injection detector. I agree with these boundaries and retain them as autonomy widens.
- **Reliability:** Retain 5 drafts/4 revisions, the 600-second run deadline, 60-second API timeout, $0.10 estimated per-run threshold, and 10-story proposal cap. Use only bounded retry policies: up to 3 attempts for missing/invalid required data, immediate STUCK on read-tool connection failures, and no automatic SDK retries for model requests. If a model/tool is unavailable, stop safely, save the failure reason, and hold incomplete outputs for human review; the PM can complete the task manually. Resume only after recovery and relevant validation. Do not automatically switch to an unvalidated model, grant permissions, or publish. I agreed because this is a good reliability approach for Cortex. Actual service availability, notification routing, and service-wide spending controls remain deployment requirements; per-run estimates alone do not cap an always-on service's total spend.
- **Strategy:** Prioritize the experienced-PM preparation workflow after the agreed gate: 6 weeks, at least 30 supervised runs, at least 95% pass for each EV-1–EV-4, 100% pass on evaluated EV-5 cases, zero unauthorized actions/disclosures, reviewed and closed unexpected issues, and explicit PM-owner approval. Experienced PMs can review complete proposals with fewer intermediate interruptions while retaining final approval over communications and scope. PMs new to Cortex remain assisted. Final claims/story approval, publication, and commitments remain human-owned. The current supervised runtime already proceeds through preparation after context/tone approval, so a future bounded-autonomous rung must first specify and implement an actual workflow change and evaluate that change; changing the label alone does not widen autonomy. No higher rung or gate attainment is claimed today.
